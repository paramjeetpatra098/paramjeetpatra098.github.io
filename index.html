AI CREATIVE WORKFLOW

Minimalist Ad Pre-flight

Generate or review a brand-safe Meta ad from verified product evidence before it leaves the tool.

LIVE APP
Product evidence→Generate or upload→Review & fix→Export

Problem

Creative teams need speed, but unsupported claims, weak evidence and brand-rule misses can turn a fast draft into risky advertising.

Product approach

A two-path workflow for creating a new ad or reviewing an existing one, with evidence-bounded generation, deterministic pre-flight scoring and export gating.

Built with

Python · Streamlit · OpenAI · versioned brand and claims rules · golden-case evaluations · privacy-safe operational logs.

Open live app ↗ View source ↗
Product notes +
  • Starts from a product URL, with manual evidence and image fallbacks when a page cannot be read.
  • Separates reference-only data such as price, ratings and offers from automatically usable ad claims.
  • Blocks creative export while unresolved REVIEW or BLOCK findings remain.
  • Keeps the rulebook, prompt snapshots and scorer evaluation fixtures versioned alongside the application.
Product lesson: Generative speed becomes useful only when evidence, evaluation and clear human control are part of the workflow.
COMMERCE AUTOMATION

Basketly

Compare live grocery prices across four retailers without a paid API or background server.

CHROME
ZeptoBlinkitInstamartFlipkart Minutes

Problem

The cheapest grocery option is difficult to find when pack sizes, product names, availability, delivery times and prices differ across retailer apps.

Product approach

A local-first Chrome extension that reads live product cards for the user’s signed-in delivery location, normalizes quantity and unit price, classifies products and keeps retailer carts as the source of truth.

Built with

Manifest V3 · JavaScript · Chrome tabs, storage, scripting and debugger APIs · store-specific extraction and cart verification.

View project ↗
Product notes +
  • Normalizes weight, volume and item count so “best price” means best comparable unit price.
  • Uses adaptive facets to group varied retailer titles by category, quantity and brand.
  • Confirms cart mutations by reading the retailer cart again instead of trusting button labels or badges.
  • Reports a store as unavailable when a session, page or selector fails rather than inventing a listing.
Product lesson: Comparison products earn trust through normalization, failure transparency and verification—not by merely aggregating more results.

Making proprietary market intelligence accessible inside the LLM ecosystem.

Problem

Market research often required users to move between search interfaces, filters and datasets. The emerging LLM ecosystem created an opportunity to make proprietary data available where users were already asking questions.

Product bet

Rather than build only another standalone chatbot, expose Tracxn capabilities through MCP so users can bring trusted market intelligence into their existing AI workflows.

My role

Product strategy, customer discovery, workflow identification, requirements, prioritization, engineering collaboration, launch and adoption.

Outcome

35% of users/accounts adopted MCP. The product became an AI-native distribution channel for proprietary market intelligence.

Read the case study +

How I approached it

  1. Start with workflows, not the technology. I focused on customer problems where proprietary data materially improved the answer rather than treating MCP as an end in itself.
  2. Define the useful surface area. Prioritize the data and capabilities that could support meaningful research workflows while keeping the experience understandable and reliable.
  3. Design for adoption. Product discovery and onboarding mattered as much as the technical integration; customers needed to understand what they could actually accomplish.
  4. Learn from usage. Adoption became a key feedback loop for deciding which workflows and capabilities deserved deeper investment.
Product lesson: For emerging AI interfaces, the hard part is rarely “can we connect the model?” The hard part is identifying a workflow valuable enough for customers to change how they work.

04 / AI OPERATING MODEL

AI-powered Product Workflows

Redesigning the product development lifecycle with LLMs.

Feedback→Manual analysis→Requirements→Testing→Reporting
Inputs→LLM analysis→Structured output→Human validation→Action

I’ve used AI/LLMs to automate or accelerate requirement gathering, customer feedback analysis, requirement writing, product testing, quality analysis, market and competitive research, and dashboard/MIS creation. The goal was not to remove human judgment; it was to move PM time away from repetitive transformation work and toward decisions, prioritization and customer outcomes.

Case study +

Human-in-the-loop by design

For product work, speed without quality is not a win. I treated LLM outputs as drafts or decision-support artifacts where appropriate, with validation steps for requirements, analysis, testing and quality-sensitive work.

What I learned

  • Good AI automation starts with a well-defined input/output contract.
  • Evaluation criteria should be explicit before automation is scaled.
  • AI is most useful when it compresses repetitive work while preserving human accountability for product decisions.

HOW I WORK

Technology is the input. Outcomes are the point.

My product approach combines customer evidence, technical depth and measurable business outcomes.

01

Start with the workflow

I look for the job customers are trying to complete before deciding what technology or feature to build.

02

Make the technical trade-off explicit

03

Design for adoption

A launch is not the finish line. Discovery, onboarding, customer education and usage loops are part of the product.

Measure business impact

I connect product decisions to adoption, usage, productivity, revenue and customer value wherever the signal is measurable.
